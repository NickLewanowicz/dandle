# Elena — beta feedback, round 1 (2026-10-17)

**Who I am:** I'm 68. I watch my grandson Milo two afternoons a week; my granddaughter Zoe goes to preschool most days. My daughter-in-law put this app on my phone for me and texted me a server address and a login. I had never opened it before today. I am careful with phones — I don't press things I can't undo, and I do not like red.

**What I did:** fresh start (my daughter-in-law "reset" it for me), logged in by myself, then logged a real afternoon: a wet diaper, a 90 mL bottle, and a nap that started before I picked up the phone. I also made the mistake I was afraid of — I hit Pumping instead of Notes — and tried to take it back. Evidence: `docs/screenshots/ux-review/elena/` (screenshots `01`–`31`).

---

## My day with it

### 1. First open, and a door that wouldn't open
Dark screen, "Baby Buddy — Track feeds, sleep, and care." Three boxes: **Server URL**, **Username**, **Password** (`01`). I know username and password. "Server URL" is nothing. I typed `admin` / `admin` and pressed Sign in like a normal person — **and nothing happened. The button just sat there grey** (`02`). No message, no shake, nothing. For a moment I was sure I'd broken it.

Only because my daughter-in-law's text had that odd number (`http://10.0.2.2:8000`) did I guess it goes in the top box (`03`). Second try, the button turned green and worked. **Honest score: login took me two tries, and the first failure taught me nothing.**

Then, before I was even in, the app asked me: **"Unencrypted connection — Your credentials and data travel in clear text. Continue?"** (`04`). Clear text? Is someone reading? Nobody warned me; it felt like I'd done something wrong just logging in the way I was told. I pressed Continue with my heart pounding.

### 2. The main screen
Six big round buttons: Feeding, Diaper, Sleep, Notes, Pumping, Tummy time (`05`). The words carry them; the pictures less so — Diaper's icon looks like a cup, and Pumping's contraption means nothing to me (it's not mine anyway). Under them, "Today" and a list with times and little lengths (3m, 20s) I decided were "how long". For a wet diaper I pressed **Diaper**.

### 3. The diaper that saved itself into thin air
The card asked exactly what I guessed: Wet or Solid, plus "Extra options" I didn't need (`06`). I tapped Wet, pressed Save. The card closed and… **my 1:29 diaper was not in the list.** The newest entry was still "Sleep 12:50." I waited, looked again (`07`, `08`) — nothing.

I checked later, quietly, with help: the diaper **did** arrive on the family computer. It went somewhere, but the list in front of me never said so. If my daughter-in-law weren't coming over, I'd have pressed Save again and logged two diapers.

### 4. The bottle, and the timer I never asked for
I pressed Feeding to say a bottle happened — and the button went green with a dot and **a clock started counting**, with a second green bar across the top also counting (`09`). Two green clocks at once, and I never said I wanted a timer; I wanted to *report* a bottle that was nearly finished already. I tapped Amount, typed 90, pressed Save (`10`). Same story as the diaper: closed, gone from the list. (Bottle · 90 mL, breast milk, Bottle method — it recorded all of it faithfully, I checked. The list just never showed me.)

### 5. The nap that started before my phone did
Milo fell asleep on the rug about twenty minutes before I picked up the phone. When I pressed Sleep, the card showed one big `00:00:00` counting from **now**, a Note box, and Save (`11`). There is no place — not even an "Extra options" arrow like Diaper had — to say "he actually went down at 1:15." My choices were to tell the phone a lie or leave it out. I did what I'd really do: let the clock run while he slept and pressed Save when he woke. So the app holds a nap with the right note and a wrong start (`12`).

When I finally met my entries (see below), it said "Sleep · nap · 13:33 · 57s". A grandmother can see that's not her afternoon; nothing in the app ever suggested fixing it.

### 6. The pump I never pumped
I reached for Notes and hit **Pumping**. It went green and RUNNING instantly (`13`) — my stomach dropped. In the fluster I pressed the big Save. Now a pump I never did was *in* the phone, and I went looking for it. I scrolled: old things, strange things someone typed ("offline one", "wire check 42 priya" — if those are tests, they still live in *my* list and they make me doubt everything else in it). **I could not find my pump anywhere** (`15`). My one trick from email — pulling the list down — did nothing I could see (`16`).

I found it by pure luck: I tapped the little "Milo" pill, peeked at Zoe's page (`19`), came back — **and the list had woken up.** There were my bottle, my diaper, my nap, and the pump (`20`, `24`-state). Everything had saved; the list just needed a shake to admit it.

Taking the pump back, once I could see it, was the best part of my day: tap the row → a card opens with the amount and the times (`21`) → scroll → a small red **Delete** beside the big green Save (`22`) → it **asked me first**: "Delete entry? This removes it from the app and the server." (`23`) → gone, instantly, from the list and the server (`24`). Un-scary and respectful — *once reachable.* Deleting updates the list right away, by the way, which makes the missing saves feel even stranger.

### 7. The children and the little dots
The "Milo" pill opens **Milo Rivera / Zoe Rivera** (`18`) — I found my granddaughter on my own. No tick marks which one is showing, but the pill itself says. Zoe's page reads like a diary — "Yesterday", "October 15", "October 14" (`19`) — that part I love. On both pages, entries like "Wet + Solid (Brown) · Brown" with a little brown ball: it tells me the color twice, once in words and once in a dot; fine by me.

### 8. Help and About
Four cards. The first ("Six buttons") would have saved me the feeding surprise if I'd read it first (`25`). The second ("Timers") explains the very thing that confuses me, in words that scare me: "mis-tap… discards quietly", "Save, Discard, and Keep" — **Discard and Keep mean opposite things and I cannot tell which is which** — and "survive restarts" sounds like the phone died (`26`). The third ("Offline first") is actually comforting — "Every save lands on this phone immediately" — but then it promises little marks called "Will sync" and "Sync failed" (`27`) that I never saw anywhere, and it tells me to long-press into my "server's web UI", which is three words I don't know. The fourth ("Widgets") says I can put buttons on my home screen; "configure" is office talk (`28`). About (`29`) is calm: "All data lives on your own server and this phone" is a nice sentence, and nothing in there is red or alarming.

### 9. Old entries
Tapping the 12:44 "Wet" opens the same card — colors, amount, even the time with little −15m/−1m/+1m/+15m buttons (`30`). Clear — but Save and Delete sit side by side at the bottom, and one slip erases a diaper. I closed it and touched nothing (`31`).

---

## Verdict: **FIX FIRST**

The bones are kind: big words I understand (Wet, Bottle, Sleep), a delete that asks permission, a page for Zoe that reads like her diary. But I pressed Save three times today and the list never once told me the truth until I accidentally shook it. A grandmother who can't trust the list will either stop using the app or double-log everything. That single break undoes every kind thing around it.

## What I need (most important first)

1. **When I press Save, let my entry appear in Today right away.** I saved a diaper, a bottle, and a nap and the list showed none of them until I switched children and back. I should never have to guess whether my afternoon made it in. (I'm told the engineers call this a known regression — from where I sit it's the whole app.)
2. **Never leave me pressing a dead button.** If Sign in needs something more, say what — in words like "the address your family's app lives at" — before I decide I broke it. And soften the "Unencrypted connection" wall: "clear text" made me feel accused; if it must be said, say it so my daughter-in-law can explain it to me first.
3. **Let me tell the truth about time.** When I tap Sleep and he's *already* asleep, give me a plain way to say when he went down — right on that first card. And if what I saved says "nap · 57s" but I clearly meant an afternoon nap, nudge me toward fixing it.
4. **Make mistakes findable.** When a save lands, a small "Saved — Undo" for a few seconds would have replaced my worst moment with a shrug. And let pulling the list down fetch what's new, like my email does — it's the one trick I know.
5. **Say it my way.** Explain "Discard" vs "Keep" with an example; call the web escape hatch "see everything on the family computer"; show me where those "Will sync" / "Sync failed" marks actually appear, because I looked and found none.
6. **Keep Delete away from Save** at the bottom of the edit card — Delete's little question saved me once already; give Save the same guard-rail feeling.

*Left the app as I found it: open on Milo's page, no timers running, the family's old entries untouched, and my accidental pump removed by its own Delete flow.*
